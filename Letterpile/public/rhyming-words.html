Rhyming Words Finder

Type a word to see every word that rhymes with it, grouped by syllables, plus near rhymes that share only the last syllable.

Rhymes appear here, shortest words first. Tap a word for its definition.

Two words rhyme when they sound the same from their last stressed vowel to the end: RHYME and TIME, TABLE and CABLE. Spelling is no guide (THOUGH and THROW rhyme; THOUGH and TOUGH don't), so this finder works from pronunciations rather than letters. It uses the CMU Pronouncing Dictionary, an open dictionary of American English pronunciations, limited to the 50,751 words that are also in our word list.

How to use it

  1. Type a word and press Find rhymes.
  2. Perfect rhymes share every sound from the last stressed vowel onward. They're grouped by syllable count, so you can pick a word that fits your meter.
  3. Near rhymes share only the last syllable's sounds. They're the fallback for words with few perfect rhymes, and songwriters use them all the time.
  4. Tap any word for its definition.

Examples

RHYME has 15 perfect rhymes in the list, including CHIME, CLIMB, CRIME and DIME, and two-syllable options like ONETIME and SUBLIME. TABLE has 13, among them ABLE, CABLE and DISABLE.

Then there are the famously hard ones. ORANGE has no perfect rhyme in the list at all, SILVER has 1, and MONTH has none. For words like these, the near rhymes are where the useful answers are.

How rhymes are matched

Each word in the pronunciation dictionary is written as a string of sounds, with each vowel marked for stress. The finder takes the sounds from the last vowel with main stress to the end of the word and looks for other words with exactly the same sounds. For TABLE, that's the “AY-bul” at the end, which is why CABLE rhymes and TABLET doesn't. Stress matters: REBEL the noun and REBEL the verb are stressed differently, and the dictionary lists the more common pronunciation first, which is the one used here.

Near rhymes are matched from the last vowel of the word, stressed or not. For TABLE, they include everyday words like ABNORMAL, ABYSMAL and ACCEPTABLE: the endings match, the stressed vowels don't. Some near rhymes work better than others, so read the list with your ear, not just your eye.

Writing with rhymes

  • Pick the line-ending word last. Find the rhyme sound first, then write the line toward it.
  • Match the syllables. A one-syllable rhyme ends a line firmly; a two- or three-syllable rhyme (a “feminine” rhyme) sounds lighter and often comic.
  • Count your meter. Our Syllable Counter checks the syllables in a whole line or verse.

Limits

Pronunciations are American English, so some rhymes won't work in other accents, and the reverse. Only the first listed pronunciation of each word is used. Words not in the pronunciation dictionary, which includes many rare words, plurals of rare words and brand-new words, can't be looked up.

Privacy

The rhyme list loads into your browser once, and lookups happen on your device.

Frequently asked questions

What's the difference between a perfect and a near rhyme?
Perfect rhymes share every sound from the last stressed vowel to the end. Near rhymes share only the last syllable's sounds, so they're looser.
Why doesn't a word that looks similar appear?
Rhyming is about sound, not spelling. Words that look alike can sound different, like THOUGH and TOUGH.
Which accent does it use?
American English, from the CMU Pronouncing Dictionary. Some rhymes differ in British, Australian or New Zealand English.
Why can't it find my word?
The word isn't in the pronunciation dictionary. Try a more common form, such as the singular.
Where do the pronunciations come from?
The CMU Pronouncing Dictionary from Carnegie Mellon University, used under its open licence; see the credit at the bottom of every page.
