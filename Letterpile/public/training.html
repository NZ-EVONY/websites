Strong word-game players aren't born with bigger vocabularies. They've drilled the small set of words that come up again and again: the two-letter words that let you play alongside other words, the three-letter words that fill tight gaps, the handful of words that use a Q without a U, and the habit of spotting every word in a rack quickly. These four drills cover exactly that, using the same open word list (ENABLE) as all our tools.

The drills

  • Two-letter quiz. You're shown a pair of letters and asked whether it's a word. Half the time it is; the rest are look-alikes that aren't. A word counts as mastered after three right answers in a row, and the quiz keeps returning to the ones you haven't mastered. There are 96 to learn.
  • Three-letter quiz. The same idea with the 962 three-letter words. This is the long game: most players learn them over weeks, a few at a time.
  • Anagram drill. You get seven letters and 60 seconds to type as many words as you can make from them, of three letters or more. When time's up you see how many you found out of how many were possible, and the longest ones you missed. From RETAINS, for example, there are 236 words to find.
  • Q without U. Flashcards for the 29 words in our list where Q isn't followed by U. Each card shows the word's letters shuffled; try to name the word, then flip the card and mark whether you knew it.

How to practice well

A little every day beats a long session once a week. Five minutes of the two-letter quiz with your morning coffee will have you knowing them all in a couple of weeks. Mix the drills: the quizzes build recognition, while the anagram drill builds the speed that matters with a game clock running.

Say words out loud, or picture them on a board, as you answer. Words are easier to remember when you can see where you'd play them. If a word keeps catching you out, look it up: tap it after the quiz tells you the answer and the word window shows its points and hooks, with a link to its definition.

Your progress

Progress is stored only in this browser's local storage: which words you've mastered, your known flashcards and your best anagram score. Nothing is sent anywhere. Clearing your browsing data, or pressing Reset my progress, starts you over.

Which words count

All drills use the ENABLE word list without its hidden words (vulgar words and slurs). Tournament and app dictionaries differ slightly: some games accept two-letter words that ENABLE doesn't have, such as QI and ZA, so check your own game's list for those.

Where to go next

The two-letter words guide explains what the odd ones mean, Words with Q and no U covers when to play them, and Anagram basics has techniques for spotting words in a rack.

Frequently asked questions

How is a word counted as mastered?
After three correct answers in a row about it. Get it wrong once and the count starts again.
Are the wrong options real words in other games?
They're letter pairs and triples that aren't in the ENABLE list. A few might be accepted by another game's dictionary.
How is the anagram drill scored?
One point per word found, from three letters up. The total possible is every word in the list that can be made from the seven letters.
Where is my progress saved?
Only in this browser's local storage. It isn't sent anywhere, and it doesn't follow you to another device.
Why aren't QI and ZA in the two-letter quiz?
They aren't in the ENABLE list this site uses, though some games accept them.
Can I reset my progress?
Yes. Press “Reset my progress” below the drills.
