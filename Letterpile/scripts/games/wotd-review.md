## Drafts waiting for approval (7)

### 2026-10-17: BIZARRE

- Meaning: Very strange or unusual, often in a way that's hard to explain. It describes things that are odd enough to make you look twice, rather than simply surprising.
- Usage: "The story took a bizarre turn in the last chapter." It's often misspelled: the word has one Z and a double R.

### 2026-10-18: QUEUE

- Meaning: A line of people or things waiting their turn; as a verb, to wait in such a line. Americans usually say "line" for people, but "queue" is everyday English in computing.
- Usage: "Your document is third in the print queue." British speakers say "queue up" where Americans say "line up."

### 2026-10-19: DOZEN

- Meaning: A group of twelve. "Dozens" on its own is an informal way of saying "a lot," and a baker's dozen is thirteen.
- Usage: "She bought a dozen eggs and used half of them in one cake."

### 2026-10-20: SYNTAX

- Meaning: The rules for how words are put together into phrases and sentences. In programming, it means the rules for how code has to be written so the computer can read it.
- Usage: "A missing bracket is a classic syntax error." The word comes from Greek words meaning "arranging together."

### 2026-10-21: UNIQUE

- Meaning: Being the only one of its kind, unlike anything else. Many careful writers avoid "very unique," since something either is one of a kind or isn't.
- Usage: "Every snowflake's pattern is said to be unique."

### 2026-10-22: JUSTIFY

- Meaning: To show that a decision or action is right or reasonable. In typesetting, it also means spacing out the words so each line lines up with both margins.
- Usage: "Can you justify the extra cost?" Newspaper columns are usually justified text.

### 2026-10-23: SQUARE

- Meaning: A shape with four equal sides and four right angles. It's also a number multiplied by itself (9 is the square of 3), an open public space in a town, and, informally, someone old-fashioned.
- Usage: "The market fills the town square every Saturday morning."
