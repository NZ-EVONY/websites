### 2026-10-24: AWKWARD

- Meaning: Clumsy or uncomfortable, either in how something moves or in how a moment feels. An awkward silence is the kind nobody knows how to break.
- Usage: "There was an awkward pause after he forgot her name." The spelling trips people up: it has a W on both sides of the K.

### 2026-10-25: FORTUNE

- Meaning: A large amount of money, or the luck, good or bad, that shapes what happens to someone. A fortune teller claims to predict it.
- Usage: "She made a small fortune selling homemade candles online." The word comes from Fortuna, the Roman goddess of luck.

### 2026-10-26: SUBTLE

- Meaning: So slight or delicate that it's easy to miss, or clever in a quiet, indirect way. A subtle flavor or a subtle hint doesn't announce itself.
- Usage: "There's a subtle difference between the two shades of blue." The B is silent: say it "SUH-tul."

### 2026-10-27: HANDY

- Meaning: Useful and easy to reach, or good at practical jobs with your hands. Something that "comes in handy" turns out to be useful later.
- Usage: "Keep a flashlight handy in case the power goes out."

### 2026-10-28: PECULIAR

- Meaning: Strange or unusual in a way that's hard to pin down. It can also mean belonging only to one person or place, as in a custom peculiar to one town.
- Usage: "The milk had a peculiar smell, so we poured it out."

### 2026-10-29: GARDEN

- Meaning: A piece of ground where flowers, fruit or vegetables are grown. As a verb, it means to look after one.
- Usage: "Every spring they plant tomatoes along the back of the garden." "Lead someone up the garden path" means to mislead them.

### 2026-10-30: MISLEAD

- Meaning: To give someone the wrong idea, whether on purpose or by accident. Its past tense is "misled," which is often misread as "MY-zuld."
- Usage: "The sign was misleading: the beach was two miles away, not two hundred yards."

### 2026-10-31: PLENTY

- Meaning: A large amount, or as much as you need and more. "Plenty of" works with things you can count and things you can't.
- Usage: "Don't worry about dinner; there's plenty of soup for everyone."

### 2026-11-01: FINITE

- Meaning: Having limits or an end; not going on forever. Its opposite, infinite, means without end.
- Usage: "The planet has a finite supply of fresh water." In grammar, a finite verb is one with a tense, like "runs" or "ran."

### 2026-11-02: CRISP

- Meaning: Firm, dry and pleasantly easy to snap, like a fresh apple or a cold morning's air. It also describes a clear, sharp style, such as a crisp white shirt.
- Usage: "The lettuce was cold and crisp straight from the fridge." In Britain, potato chips are called crisps.

### 2026-11-03: VAGUE

- Meaning: Not clear or specific; hard to make out or pin down. Vague answers leave out the details that matter.
- Usage: "He gave a vague promise to call sometime next week." It rhymes with "plague."

### 2026-11-04: GENUINE

- Meaning: Real and truly what it claims to be, or sincere and honest in feeling. A genuine smile isn't put on for show.
- Usage: "The jeweler confirmed the pearls were genuine." It's said "JEN-yoo-in," not "-ine."

### 2026-11-05: PLANET

- Meaning: A large body that travels around a star, like Earth around the Sun. The word comes from a Greek word for "wanderer," because planets seem to wander across the night sky.
- Usage: "Mars is the fourth planet from the Sun."

### 2026-11-06: RUMOR

- Meaning: A story passed from person to person without anyone knowing whether it's true. British English spells it "rumour."
- Usage: "A rumor spread that the school would close early on Friday."

### 2026-11-07: CAPABLE

- Meaning: Able to do something, or skilled enough to handle things well. It's usually followed by "of," as in "capable of anything."
- Usage: "She's a capable leader who stays calm under pressure."

### 2026-11-08: ERASER

- Meaning: A small block of rubber or plastic used to rub out pencil marks. A whiteboard eraser wipes away marker instead.
- Usage: "The pencil's eraser had worn down to the metal."

### 2026-11-09: DRASTIC

- Meaning: Strong, sudden and far-reaching, often in response to a serious problem. Drastic measures go well beyond a small fix.
- Usage: "The company made drastic cuts after losing its biggest customer."

### 2026-11-10: AMUSE

- Meaning: To make someone smile or laugh, or to keep them pleasantly busy. Something amusing is mildly funny rather than hilarious.
- Usage: "The puppy's attempts to catch its own tail amused the whole family."

### 2026-11-11: OBSCURE

- Meaning: Not well known, or hard to see or understand. As a verb, it means to hide or block from view.
- Usage: "The quiz question was about an obscure band from the 1970s." Clouds obscured the moon.

### 2026-11-12: GRATEFUL

- Meaning: Feeling or showing thanks for something someone has done. "Grateful to" a person, "grateful for" a thing.
- Usage: "I'm grateful for all the help with the move." Don't confuse it with "great": it comes from a Latin word meaning "pleasing."

### 2026-11-13: PUNCTURE

- Meaning: A small hole made by something sharp, especially in a tire. As a verb, it means to make such a hole, or to deflate someone's pride.
- Usage: "A nail in the road caused a puncture in the front tire."

### 2026-11-14: LIABLE

- Meaning: Legally responsible for something, or likely to do or suffer something. "Liable to" often warns of a risk.
- Usage: "The owner is liable for any damage the dog causes." The path is liable to flood after heavy rain.

### 2026-11-15: GATHER

- Meaning: To bring together into one place, or to come together as a group. It can also mean to understand from what you've heard.
- Usage: "Friends gathered around the fire to tell stories." I gather the meeting has been moved.

### 2026-11-16: NAIVE

- Meaning: Showing a lack of experience, so too ready to trust or believe things. It can describe a person, a plan or an idea.
- Usage: "It was naive to think the project would be done in a week." It's said "nye-EEV."

### 2026-11-17: TRIVIAL

- Meaning: Small, unimportant and not worth much attention. It comes from a Latin word for a crossroads, where people stopped to swap everyday gossip.
- Usage: "Don't let a trivial mistake ruin your whole day."

### 2026-11-18: ESCAPE

- Meaning: To get away from a place or a situation, or to avoid something unpleasant. As a noun, it's the act of getting away.
- Usage: "The cat made its escape through the open window." The Esc key on a keyboard is short for escape.

### 2026-11-19: WEIRD

- Meaning: Strange or unusual, sometimes in a slightly unsettling way. It's a famous exception to "I before E except after C."
- Usage: "We heard a weird noise coming from the attic last night."

### 2026-11-20: MERIT

- Meaning: The quality of being good or deserving praise. As a verb, it means to deserve something.
- Usage: "Each idea will be judged on its merit, not on who suggested it." The story merits a second read.

### 2026-11-21: CENTURY

- Meaning: A period of one hundred years. It's also a score of one hundred runs in cricket.
- Usage: "The bridge has stood for more than a century." The twenty-first century began in 2001.

### 2026-11-22: FEASIBLE

- Meaning: Possible to do, or practical and likely to work. A feasible plan is one you could actually carry out.
- Usage: "Building the treehouse by the weekend didn't seem feasible."

### 2026-11-23: SENSIBLE

- Meaning: Showing good judgment and common sense; practical rather than showy. Sensible shoes are made for comfort, not style.
- Usage: "Bringing an umbrella was a sensible choice given the forecast."

### 2026-11-24: BUCKET

- Meaning: An open container with a handle, used for carrying water and other things. A bucket list is a list of things to do in your lifetime.
- Usage: "The kids filled a bucket with shells at the beach." When it rains heavily, people say it's coming down in buckets.

### 2026-11-25: IRRITATE

- Meaning: To annoy someone, or to make part of the body sore or itchy. Something irritating keeps bothering you.
- Usage: "The scratchy label on my sweater irritates my neck."

### 2026-11-26: PARALLEL

- Meaning: Running side by side and always the same distance apart, like railroad tracks. It also describes things that are alike or happen at the same time.
- Usage: "The two streets run parallel to each other." The double L comes in the middle, not at the end.

### 2026-11-27: MAGNETIC

- Meaning: Able to attract iron or steel, the way a magnet does. It also describes a person with a strong, attractive personality.
- Usage: "We stuck the shopping list to the fridge with a magnetic clip."

### 2026-11-28: APOLOGY

- Meaning: A statement saying you're sorry for something you did. Its plural is "apologies," and the verb is "apologize."
- Usage: "He wrote a short apology for missing the meeting."

### 2026-11-29: COMPOSE

- Meaning: To create a piece of music, writing or art, or to calm yourself. Something "composed of" parts is made up of them.
- Usage: "Mozart began to compose music when he was five." She took a deep breath to compose herself.

### 2026-11-30: FRACTION

- Meaning: A part of a whole, written in math as one number over another, like 1/2. It also means a small part of anything.
- Usage: "The new phone costs a fraction of the old one's price."

### 2026-12-01: VIRTUE

- Meaning: A good quality in a person, such as honesty or kindness. "By virtue of" means because of.
- Usage: "Patience is a virtue, especially when you're waiting for bread to rise."

### 2026-12-02: DITTO

- Meaning: The same again; used to say that something just said applies to you too. On a list, two small marks show "ditto" instead of repeating a word.
- Usage: "I'm tired and hungry." "Ditto." It comes from an Italian word meaning "said."

### 2026-12-03: MASSIVE

- Meaning: Very large, heavy or serious in size or amount. Massive stars are many times heavier than the Sun.
- Usage: "A massive wave crashed over the sea wall."

### 2026-12-04: PLEASANT

- Meaning: Enjoyable, agreeable or friendly. A pleasant surprise is a happy one you weren't expecting.
- Usage: "We had a pleasant walk along the river after lunch."

### 2026-12-05: FLAVOR

- Meaning: The taste of a food or drink, or a quality that gives something its character. British English spells it "flavour."
- Usage: "Strawberry is her favorite ice cream flavor."

### 2026-12-06: DEDUCE

- Meaning: To work something out by reasoning from the facts you have. Detectives deduce who did it from the clues.
- Usage: "From the muddy boots by the door, she deduced that he'd been in the garden."

### 2026-12-07: LORRY

- Meaning: A large truck that carries goods by road. It's the usual word in British English, where Americans say "truck."
- Usage: "A lorry full of hay blocked the narrow country lane."

### 2026-12-08: FASHION

- Meaning: A popular style of clothing or behavior at a particular time. "In a fashion" means in a way, though not very well.
- Usage: "Wide-leg jeans came back into fashion last year."

### 2026-12-09: ULTIMATE

- Meaning: Last or final, or the best or most extreme of its kind. The ultimate goal is the one everything else leads to.
- Usage: "Winning the championship was their ultimate goal." He calls it the ultimate grilled cheese sandwich.

### 2026-12-10: DECENT

- Meaning: Good enough or of an acceptable standard, or honest and respectable. A decent person treats others fairly.
- Usage: "The hotel was small, but the beds were decent." It's said "DEE-sent," unlike "descent."

### 2026-12-11: SPITE

- Meaning: A wish to hurt or annoy someone. "In spite of" means despite, or without being stopped by.
- Usage: "They went ahead with the picnic in spite of the rain."

### 2026-12-12: ANALOG

- Meaning: Using a continuous signal or moving parts instead of digital numbers, like a clock with hands. British English often spells it "analogue."
- Usage: "My grandfather still prefers an analog watch to a smartwatch."

### 2026-12-13: CLEVER

- Meaning: Quick to learn and understand, or showing skill and new ideas. A clever trick is one that works neatly.
- Usage: "That was a clever way to fit all the boxes in the car."

### 2026-12-14: FAMILIAR

- Meaning: Well known from long or close experience. "Familiar with" means knowing about something.
- Usage: "The song sounded familiar, but I couldn't remember its name."

### 2026-12-15: MIDNIGHT

- Meaning: Twelve o'clock at night, the moment one day ends and the next begins. "Burning the midnight oil" means working late.
- Usage: "The fireworks started at the stroke of midnight."

### 2026-12-16: VITAL

- Meaning: Absolutely necessary or very important. It also means full of energy and life.
- Usage: "Water is vital to every living thing." A doctor checks your vital signs, like pulse and breathing.

### 2026-12-17: PRONE

- Meaning: Likely to suffer from or do something, usually something bad. It also means lying flat, face down.
- Usage: "This old car is prone to breaking down in cold weather."

### 2026-12-18: BULLETIN

- Meaning: A short official news report or announcement. A bulletin board is where notices are pinned up.
- Usage: "The weather bulletin warned of strong winds overnight."

### 2026-12-19: RUBBISH

- Meaning: Things thrown away as worthless; trash. In British English it also means nonsense.
- Usage: "Please put the rubbish out before the truck comes on Tuesday." "That's rubbish!" means "That's not true!"

### 2026-12-20: OBTAIN

- Meaning: To get something, especially through effort or by asking. It's a more formal word than "get."
- Usage: "You'll need to obtain a permit before building the shed."

### 2026-12-21: WINTER

- Meaning: The coldest season of the year, between fall and spring. North of the equator, its shortest day falls around December 21.
- Usage: "The pond freezes over every winter." To winter somewhere is to spend the cold months there.

### 2026-12-22: HOLIDAY

- Meaning: A day of celebration or rest when many people don't work. In British English, it also means a vacation.
- Usage: "The office is closed for the holiday, so we're baking cookies instead."
